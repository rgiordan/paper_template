
\begin{assu}\label{assu:assumptions}
A Poincare inequality and perturbation in $L2$ and stuff
\end{assu}



\begin{thm}\label{thm:existence}[Previous author citations]
    Under \cref{assu:assumptions}, there is a solution to the PDE.
\end{thm}

I just love \cref{thm:existence}.  We use the Petrov-Galerkin approximation.

\begin{thm}\label{thm:consistency}
    As the number of basis functions and test functions goes to infinity
    you converge to the solution.
\end{thm}


\begin{assu}\label{assu:bclt}
    Bernstein-von Mises like conditions.
\end{assu}

When you have a BCLT it's better.

\begin{thm}\label{thm:hermite}
    Under \cref{assu:bclt}
    he Hermite polynomials give the best approximation the the inverse in operator norm.
\end{thm}

So when you have a bootstrap perturbation (parametric or nonparametric)
and a BCLT then

\begin{assu}\label{assu:hermite}
    Let a function of interest be expandable in the Hermite polynomials
    with such-and-such coefficient decay.
\end{assu}


\begin{thm}\label{thm:bootstrap}
    Under \cref{assu:bclt,assu:hermite} the second-order Hermite polynomial flow
    is accurate asymptotically for bootstrap perturbations and expectations
    of a function of interest.
\end{thm}

\begin{cor}\label{cor:smooth}
    Smooth functions of a countable number of functions of interest are also consistent
    as in \cref{thm:bootstrap}.
\end{cor}
